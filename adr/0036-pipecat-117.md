# ADR-0036: pipecat 1.17 — one workaround out, one failover bug in

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-25
**Supersedes:** nothing. Follows ADR-0028 (each pipecat minor on purpose) and
ADR-0033 (pipecat 1.16). PRD #498, ticket #499.

## Context

pipecat 1.17.0 came out on 2026-11-22. Before the spec: every one of
TurnCall's pipecat imports exists in it, no private name or signature TurnCall
relies on changed, none of its six deprecations or three removals is used, and
the hermetic suite passed 4,252 of 4,254. The two failures were the
realtime-appends canary for `openai` and `xai`, which said that workaround
could go.

The spec named this ADR-0034; that number went to #487 (open a call, run a
call) first.

## Decision

Bump to `>=1.17.0,<1.18.0`, whole, with no behaviour change beyond what
pipecat forces, as ADR-0019, 0028, 0029 and 0033 did. New features are #498's
items.

**1. Deleted: `realtime_appends.py` (#388, #390).** With
`realtime_service_mode` (set on every S2S pair) 1.17's user aggregator pushes
an `LLMMessagesAppendFrame` on instead of folding it into a context frame, and
every realtime service but gpt-live-1 takes the messages from the frame
(OpenAI Realtime, Grok Voice and Eleven Realtime as `conversation.item.create`,
plus `response.create` when `run_llm`; Gemini Live as `send_client_content`;
Nova Sonic as a text event). `tests/unit/test_s2s_appends.py` replaces the
canary: it runs the factory's own S2S pipeline between the two aggregators
with only the socket faked, fails on 1.16 and passes on 1.17. The live xAI
tools test passes without the workaround. gpt-live-1 ignores the frame as it
ignored the context before, and its check-in goes through `say()` anyway.

Every processor TurnCall puts between the aggregators on S2S (caller speech
timer, resamplers, transcript taps, `HangupAfterReply`, the avatar stage,
`SpeechBeforeAmbience`, the recorder) was checked against seeing an append
where it used to see a context frame: none read either, all pass it on, and
the test pins that for the stages a default agent does not build.

**2. Kept, each with the test that decides it.**

- `webrtc_offer.py` (#398): `test_webrtc_offer.py::test_pipecat_still_needs_it`
  passes on 1.17.
- `SpeechBeforeAmbience` (#265): `test_ambience_bot_speech.py::test_pipecat_still_needs_it`
  passes.
- `SpeechForTheAvatar` (#401). 1.17's HeyGen and Tavus take gpt-live-1's
  `SpeechOutputAudioRawFrame`, but HeyGen ends a reply only on a gap in
  frames, and gpt-live-1 streams silence without one: the reply would never
  end and its silence would be sent to the avatar. `test_avatar_s2s.py`.
- The #257 delegation idle hold. 1.17 holds the idle timer for background
  work announced by `BackgroundWorkStartedFrame`, which only async function
  calls and `LLMWithBackend` announce; `services/openai/live` is byte-identical
  to 1.16 and its client delegation announces none.
  `test_delegation_idle_hold.py::test_pipecat_still_needs_it` builds the real
  gpt-live-1 service, starts a delegation and sees 1.17's idle controller fire
  mid-delegation; it fails once pipecat announces the work, and then the early
  return in `CallSession._arm_idle_guard` and `TestADelegateAtWork` go.
  `DelegationTrackingLiveService.delegating` stays either way: #413's
  unended-goodbye wait reads it.

**3. Fixed: TTS failover with a voice that synthesizes ahead.** 1.17 turns
ElevenLabs' `pause_frame_processing` off, so it synthesizes the next reply
while the last one is still coming out, as Cartesia already did. `FailoverTTS`
kept one reply, which lost a reply on a stall (a later reply's input made the
earlier one's audio count as heard; an earlier silent one replayed only the
later), and a written-off voice's already-synthesized audio for a later reply
still came out beside the backup's (the switcher never gates a member's
downstream output), so the caller heard it twice. It now keeps every reply
handed over and not yet played out, replays them all oldest first, and drops a
written-off voice's frames by context id. `test_tts_failover_concurrent_contexts.py`
runs the real 1.17 ElevenLabs service against a scripted socket; the old code
failed 2 of its 5 tests on 1.16 and 1.17. Pinning `pause_frame_processing=True`
was rejected: it gives up the gapless replies and leaves Cartesia's double
speech.

**4. Checked, no change: the assistant aggregator's order on an
interruption.** 1.17 closes an interrupted turn behind the text frames still
queued, so its context frames now follow the `InterruptionFrame`, and the words
already played stay in that turn instead of starting the next. The procedure
window and `MoveAfterSpeech` sit upstream of the aggregator and share its
context; `replace_turn_message` re-reads it and finds the turn by identity.
`test_procedures_across_a_barge_in.py` runs the real aggregator pair around
both, barge-ins, cancelled and surviving tool calls included, and passes on
1.16 and 1.17.

**5. Measured, neutral: Deepgram's finalize confirmation.** 1.17 forwards
Deepgram's empty finalize answer as a `TranscriptionFinalizedFrame`, so a turn
whose text Deepgram had already sent no longer waits out the STT safety net
(`DEEPGRAM_TTFS_P99`, 0.35 s after the caller's silence). Replayed on 75
recorded Deepgram calls (63.8 min of caller audio, real time, TurnCall's own
STT, VAD and Smart Turn builders, 1.16 twice and 1.17 once side by side):

| run | turns | `turn_end_ms` p50 |
|---|---|---|
| 1.16 (a) | 356 | 415 |
| 1.16 (b) | 353 | 424 |
| 1.17 | 354 | 428 |

Paired by the caller's silence, the median difference is 0 ms for 1.16 against
1.16 and for 1.16 against 1.17, with the same spread (p5/p95 about ±65 ms) and
turns split, merged or cut off in 15 calls in every comparison. The
confirmation arrives about 0.45 s after the silence (VAD's 0.2 s, then the
`Finalize` round trip, ~0.21 s of it network from where it was measured),
after the safety net has already released the turn. A deployment near
Deepgram could gain up to ~0.1 s (estimated) on turns Smart Turn judged
complete. Nothing in TurnCall changes.

## The realtime tracing spans change (pipecat #6258)

S2S calls only; cascade spans and the `conversation` → `turn` spans are
unchanged, and no TurnCall code reads spans (`metrics.turn` comes from the
latency observer). What a dashboard must change:

- `llm_response` is one span per model reply, from the end of the caller's
  speech to the reply's end (or the barge-in that cut it), exported up to ~2 s
  late. It was a millisecond span around one event handler. Reply latency is
  `metrics.ttfb` / `gen_ai.response.time_to_first_chunk`; barge-ins
  `llm.interrupted`; the caller's transcript is its `input`. Gone: OpenAI's
  `response.*` and `function_calls.*` attributes, Gemini's `turn_complete`.
- `llm_tool_call` is one span per function call, lasting the tool's run, with
  `gen_ai.tool.*` attributes and the result. It replaces Gemini's
  `llm_tool_call` (`tool.*`) and `llm_tool_result`, and is new on OpenAI
  Realtime, Eleven Realtime, Grok Voice and Nova Sonic.
- `stt` (OpenAI Realtime, Gemini Live) and `llm_request` (OpenAI Realtime) are
  gone. Nova Sonic and Grok Voice gain spans; gpt-live-1 still has none.
- `gen_ai.provider.name` now comes from the service's class name, and pipecat
  maps only its own. TurnCall's subclasses report `handoffgeminilive` (was
  `gcp.gemini`), `elevenrealtime` (was `openai`), `keyawaregrokrealtime` and
  `_refreshingnovasonic`; `openai` is unchanged. Filter on these or on
  `gen_ai.request.model`. Normalising the value (renaming the classes, or a
  span processor) is possible later; the bump does not do it.

Redaction needs nothing: `telemetry_redaction` redacts every string attribute
of a redacting project's spans, whatever the span or key, so the new carriers
of caller text (`input`, `output`, tool arguments and results) are covered.

## Behaviour that changes with the bump, accepted

- An interrupted turn keeps the words the caller heard; they no longer start
  the next assistant message.
- The idle timer no longer fires while a function call runs, an async one
  included.
- ElevenLabs synthesizes replies back to back on one socket (section 3).
- Cartesia splits sentences by the configured language, not English;
  ElevenLabs and Cartesia word timestamps stay in step across replies.
- Evals: a scripted turn's pauses may total at most 60 s, so a stored scenario
  over it no longer parses; a judge that fails twice is `judge_no_verdict` with
  the error in its reason (#501 maps it to `errored`); a harness service failing
  after the last turn or before bot-ready is an error, not a pass; audio
  simulations wait for the bot's whole reply.
- `FastAPIWebsocketTransport` logs a close after the peer left at DEBUG, not
  ERROR (carrier media streams, the eval bot server).

## Follow-ups

- **Live `message` on S2S (#519).** `live_control.message_handler` still
  refuses it with a 409, for a reason 1.17 removed: the realtime services now
  take an append into the session. The bump changes only what 1.17 forces, so
  lifting the refusal (gpt-live-1 excepted) is its own ticket.
- #498's items, in its build order: #500 gpt-live-1.1, #501 and #502 eval
  verdicts and judge transcription, #503 Flux with Smart Turn, #504 cancellable
  async tools, #505 wait reassurance (which, for gpt-live-1, needs
  `delegating` until pipecat announces delegation work), #507 multilingual
  calls, #508 withholding (ADR-0035), #510 usage, #512 Rime Prism, #513 SIP.
- Tracing a gpt-live-1 client delegate into the call's trace: 1.17's
  `BackendLLMWorker` forwards `enable_tracing` and `conversation_id`. Not done.
